Scientific vision

The decision is part of the scientific problem

An explanation can change a choice. An uncertain estimate can still look convincing. An intervention that works today may alter what is needed tomorrow. These connections motivate my research programme.

I develop AI methods and examine their role in decisions, connecting technical evaluation with questions of appropriate reliance, changing patient context, and longer-term outcomes. Healthcare makes these questions concrete: treatment choices, response assessment, and support for patients between clinical visits.

Looking ahead · 2027–2031

A research agenda for
AI-supported human decisions

I aim to connect methodological development with evaluation of how AI changes decisions in practice.

  1. 01

    Explanations that support appropriate reliance

    Investigate which forms of explanation help people recognize limitations, compare alternatives, and respond appropriately when AI advice is misleading or uncertain.

    Research question: what should an explanation change in the decision?

  2. 02

    Adaptive support under partial observability

    Develop sequential decision methods that account for imperfect estimates of patient state, changing engagement, and the longer-term effects of intervention timing.

    Research question: how should uncertainty in patient state influence the next action?

  3. 03

    Evaluation across models, people, and outcomes

    Bring uncertainty assessment and human decision studies together, working towards prospective clinical evaluation that connects technical behaviour with choices and patient-relevant outcomes.

    Research question: when does a better model lead to a better decision?

Research motivation

Working across the methodological
and the human questions

I am motivated by decisions whose consequences matter to people. My experience spans industrial medical AI, digital health, and academic clinical research. Across these settings, the recurring question is how an algorithm becomes part of someone’s judgement or everyday life.

I value collaborations where clinical questions help shape the methods, and methodological questions can reshape how a problem is studied. I bring experience in project development, supervision, and the design of patient-support systems.
