The next decision
under uncertainty

How can AI help us choose and explain the next step when the evidence about a patient is uncertain?

I connect explainability, uncertainty estimation, and reinforcement learning to study decisions over time: what we infer about a patient, what support or treatment comes next, and how AI influences that choice.

The scientific problem

We make decisions about a state
we can only partly observe

Images, wearable signals, and patient reports provide observations, not a complete account of a person’s condition. Measurements vary, interpretations differ, and the effects of an action unfold over time.

My research asks how to represent that uncertainty, choose the next action, and communicate the evidence behind it. The endpoint is a decision: does AI help someone act appropriately, including when its advice is wrong?

Three connected research pillars

Understanding, reliance, and action

Explanations inform how people interpret evidence. Uncertainty shapes when to rely on it. Sequential decision-making examines what to do next.

01 · Explainability

What drives a prediction?

Investigating whether explanations help clinicians recognise misleading advice. Clinical meaning, agreement with reader attention, and faithfulness to model behaviour are separate questions.

Explore explainability research ↗

02 · Sequential decision-making

Which action, and when?

Learning which support to offer and when, given changing context and previous responses. Engagement, adherence, and health outcomes need different objectives and evaluation.

Explore personalized interventions ↗

03 · Uncertainty

How much should we rely on AI?

Estimating patient state from incomplete and variable observations. Uncertainty affects both our assessment at one visit and the changes we infer between visits.

Looking ahead · 2027–2031

A research agenda for
AI-supported human decisions

I aim to connect methodological development with evaluation of how AI changes decisions in practice.

  1. 01

    Explanations that support appropriate reliance

    Compare explanations with radiologists’ gaze and diagnostic reasoning, while separately testing faithfulness to model behaviour. Evaluate effects on diagnostic choices, reading time, confidence, and burden, especially when AI advice is wrong.

    Research question: can an explanation help a clinician detect a mistake, rather than make wrong advice more persuasive?

  2. 02

    Adaptive support under partial observability

    Develop sequential decision methods that account for imperfect estimates of patient state, changing engagement, and the longer-term effects of intervention timing.

    Research question: how should uncertainty in patient state influence the next action?

  3. 03

    Evaluation across models, people, and outcomes

    Bring uncertainty assessment and human decision studies together, working towards prospective clinical evaluation that connects technical behaviour with choices and patient-relevant outcomes.

    Research question: when does a better model lead to a better decision?

My role & motivation

A bridge between AI research
and cancer care

At VU Computer Science and Cancer Center Amsterdam UMC, my role is to develop joint research questions, build projects, and supervise work across disciplines. Patient-facing reinforcement learning and clinician-facing imaging provide complementary settings for the same scientific questions.

My route here began with eye tracking, continued through industrial medical AI and personalised health support, and returned to imaging through clinical collaborations. It taught me to ask not only whether a model predicts well, but whether we are measuring and optimising the outcome that matters.
